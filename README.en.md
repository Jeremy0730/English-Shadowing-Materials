# English Shadowing Materials

Listening is not the same as speaking. This repo is my own shadowing shelf: podcasts I have actually watched, broken into lines I can say out loud, practiced the same way every day.

## Why this repo exists

I was studying English every day and my speaking still did not move. Native speakers were running. I was walking, and sometimes I stopped. Watching and listening trained my ears. They did not train my mouth.

So I kept the episodes I had watched over the past half year on [Speak English with David & Alice](https://www.youtube.com/@English.Academy.plus-o). The speech is clear, mostly B1–B2 daily conversation, and it is good for shadowing. Each episode is one markdown file, named after that episode, directly in `Speak English with David & Alice/`. One line, one breath. Listen once, then read the line out loud. If you get stuck, go back.

Files are numbered from the newest watch to the oldest. I shadow a short clip many times. I do not shadow a full 40-minute episode in one sitting.

## How to shadow

These steps are taken from David and Alice's episode [How to Practice English Shadowing (Step by Step) | Stop Just Listening. Start Speaking Along!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BAD3cs0aFCo)

Shadowing is a speaking technique. You listen to someone speaking clear English, and you speak with them at the same time, or one or two seconds behind. You follow the speaker the way a shadow follows a body. Listening alone is not enough. Your ears can understand a sentence while your mouth still has to learn the sounds, the movement, and the rhythm. Shadowing trains both at once.

Three things happen together:

1. **Pronunciation.** You copy the real sounds. You are not reading a book and guessing.
2. **Rhythm and speed.** English has a music. Some words are fast, some are slow, some are stressed. Shadowing gives you that music.
3. **Confidence.** After many repetitions, the phrases become automatic. You do not think, then speak. You just speak. It is like driving: at first you think about every move, and later your hands already know.

You do not need a teacher or a classroom. A phone or a computer, and a clear recording, are enough. At home, in the car, on the bus.

### 1. Choose the right material

The wrong audio can waste the whole practice. Look for all of these:

- Clear speech. Not too fast, and not a very strong accent. Do not start with a fast song or with rap. English-learning podcasts and videos work well. This episode itself is good material.
- A little above your level. Too easy, and you learn nothing. Too hard, and you repeat sounds you do not understand. Your brain gets frustrated instead of learning. Walk first, then jog, then run.
- Something you enjoy.

### 2. Listen first. Do not repeat yet

Listen once or twice and understand what you are about to say. Read the recipe before you cook. If there are subtitles or a transcript, use them. Read while you listen, so the sounds connect to the words.

On this pass, notice three things:

- **Speed.** Is the speaker comfortable for you?
- **Pronunciation.** Which sounds are new or difficult?
- **Rhythm and tone.** Which words are stressed? Where are the pauses? Where does the voice go up, and where does it go down?

### 3. Start shadowing

Play the audio and speak with the speaker, or one or two seconds behind. If you wait five seconds and then repeat, that is repetition, not shadowing. A shadow stays close.

While you shadow:

- You will miss words, mispronounce words, and lose your place. That is normal. Do not stop for every mistake. Keep going and catch up.
- Do not think about the meaning. For these few minutes, be a parrot. Copy the sounds, the music, and the rhythm. The meaning comes with repetition.
- Speak out loud. Repeating silently in your head is not shadowing. A quiet voice or a whisper is fine, as long as your mouth moves. Speaking is physical. You cannot train your mouth by thinking.

### 4. Shadow without the text

In step 2 the text helps you understand. In this step, close it. The transcript is a set of training wheels: use it first, then take it off.

Without the text, you have to listen more carefully and hold the sounds in memory for about a second before you say them. Real conversations do not come with subtitles. This step is closer to real speaking.

Keep the clip short: about **30 seconds to 1 minute**. Do not try to shadow a 10-minute video in one go at the beginning. Shadowing 30 seconds ten times is better than shadowing 10 minutes once.

### 5. Record yourself

Record one take and listen back. While you are speaking, your voice in your head sounds fine. The recording is what other people hear.

Play the original, then play your recording. Compare the sounds, the rhythm, and the speed. You might hear a wrong sound, such as *th*, a pace that is too slow, or flat intonation. The original speaker is the model you are trying to match.

Do not delete the recordings. After one or two months, play an old one next to a new one. On days when it feels like nothing is changing, the recordings show that it is.

### One session

About 10 to 15 minutes. Picture 7:00 a.m., coffee on the table, phone open:

1. Choose about 30 seconds of audio at your level, something you like.
2. Listen once. Use subtitles or a transcript if you have them, and understand what it is about.
3. Listen again. This time hear the sounds, the rhythm, the speed, and where the voice rises and falls.
4. Speak one or two seconds behind. If you miss a word, keep going and finish the 30 seconds.
5. Close the text and shadow again.
6. Record one more time. Listen back and compare it with the original.

The steps are not the hard part. Starting is, and so is showing up the next day. If you do this every day, you can hear a difference yourself in **two to four weeks**: cleaner pronunciation, better rhythm, speech that feels more natural.

### Four mistakes

People try shadowing, do it the wrong way, and then say it does not work. The technique is not the problem.

1. **Material that is too hard.** Faster and more complicated does not mean faster progress. Do not run a marathon on the first day.
2. **Doing it only once.** Repeat the same clip at least five times, and up to ten, until it feels easy and you do not have to think. Do not change the material every day. Shadow the same 30 seconds many times.
3. **Practicing while distracted.** A phone, another video, or a conversation beside you will break it. Especially at the beginning, give the sounds your full attention.
4. **Not speaking out loud.** The mouth, the tongue, and the lips have to practice the sounds.

### How often

How often matters more than how long. Ten minutes every day is better than two hours on Saturday. Water the plant a little every day. Do not flood it once a week. If you practice only once a week, most of it is gone before the next time.

- Beginners: **10 to 15 minutes a day.** With morning coffee, on the bus, or before bed.
- Later, you can go to 20 or 30 minutes. Even then, 15 to 20 minutes of focused shadowing a day is enough.
- Ten focused minutes are worth more than an hour of distracted listening.
- Practice when you are most awake. Morning works for many people. If you are sharper at night, do it at night. The best time is the time you will actually do it.

### A 7-day challenge

Pick any 30 seconds of this episode that you like. Shadow that same clip every day for seven days. On the seventh day, record yourself and listen.
